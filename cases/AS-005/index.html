How to look at this group of works

An animated botanical scene combining oil-paint-like broken brushwork with a romantic fantasy atmosphere and Impressionist-like color tendencies

Look first at the recognizable volume of the flowers and the small, broken, opaque strokes of color, then at the cool-colored night and warm glimmers. During playback, petals and foliage continuously twist in localized areas; this is closer to the original reference than simply overlaying raindrops on a static garden. Its botanical subject matter overlaps with the existing decorative illustrations, while the painted surface, nighttime glow and animated deformation make it a distinct case.

“Oil painting” and “Impressionist tendencies” describe visible texture and color associations; the original post has not confirmed the actual medium, production software, use of AI or attribution to the original artist. Light and atmosphere

Animated deformation

Across the 13 frames, petals, clusters of leaves and localized brush marks continuously stretch, fold and twist in liquid undulations resembling water ripples; the main flowers and plant areas remain recognizable. This describes the visible movement, without identifying a particular filter or software from it.

Rain and short loops

Fine, nearly vertical rain streaks change position, and golden points of light flicker between frames; the framing, overall composition and viewpoint stay fixed, with no cuts. The platform file is approximately 0.91 seconds long and contains 13 frames; the short loop creates continuous quivering and shifting light.

Choices when reusing these features

First transfer the opaque broken brushwork, the relationship between dense detail and open space, the cool dark areas and the localized warm highlights. For animation, then add controlled local water-ripple deformation, fine rain and flickering light; people’s identities and important objects must remain stable.

Tendencies to avoid

Avoid directly adding the reference’s sea of flowers to an arbitrary photograph; do not replace the painted surface with uniform noise, intensely glowing spots or overall blur. When transferring the animation, avoid damaging faces and important objects with liquid distortion, and do not mistake local deformation for camera shake.

English

Add a short looping animation to the completed repaint. Keep the camera, framing and overall composition fixed, with no cuts, zooms or camera moves. Give existing plants, textiles or background brushwork continuous, controlled, localized water-ripple-like movement: gentle stretching, folding and swaying, while keeping the main subject’s contours and position readable. Keep faces, body structures and important objects stable; do not add entities or change object counts. Add fine, low-contrast, nearly vertical rain streaks to the environment, along with golden glimmers of varied spacing and subtly varying brightness; keep these animated accents restrained and do not obscure the main subject. Preserve the existing painted brushwork, cool dark areas and soft petal-like highlights. Join the end and beginning of the loop naturally, creating a breathing rhythm through rain, glimmers and local deformation together. Avoid camera shake, global deformation, strobing, motion trails and excessive glow.

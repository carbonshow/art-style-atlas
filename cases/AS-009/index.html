Reference case · AS-009

Contemporary Chinese-style Illustration · Landscapes Inspired by Classical Poetry

Artist unverified

This group places a moment from a classical Chinese poem within a narrow, elongated frame. Dense canopies surround a river, path or white wall, and the eye moves slowly along pale openings before resting on solitary fishing, a pointing gesture, a moored boat, a dragonfly on a lotus tip or a flowering branch reaching over a wall. Its strength is the progression from large color areas to small narratives to fine texture, creating different experiences at a distance and up close. Blue-green and vermilion are the most striking colors, but blue-gray snow scenes, peach-pink blossom fields and green lotus ponds show how the same compositional language can carry different seasons. As posters, the series is coherent and its subjects are easy to read; as poetic illustrations, the splendor and exuberance of some images can also diminish the chill, melancholy or contrast between past and present in the poems.

How to look at this group of works

Contemporary Chinese-style decorative landscape illustration: classical-poetry narratives, blue-green and vermilion color, fine outlines, paper-like grain and scroll-like open space

First find a continuous pale opening; then see whether clustered canopies, near-black branches and finely outlined buildings enclose it on both sides; finally, find the small action carrying the poem’s meaning. Compared with AS-008’s varied flat patterns, this group relies more on complete landscape scenes, rivers and roads as visual routes, and open space in the layout. Compared with AS-001’s gold-leaf decoration, it organizes images with ink lines, matte color areas and paper-like texture. “Contemporary Chinese-style illustration” is used as a broad category of contemporary design; this group is further described as decorative landscape and classical-poetry narrative. Blue-green and vermilion colors, scroll-like open space, fine outlines and paper-like grain are specific features. “Scroll-like” describes the viewing rhythm: all attachments are vertical posters. Appearance alone cannot establish a traditional school of painting, actual woodblock-printing technique or AI generation.

All eight images are user-provided reference attachments. Original bytes, order, full frames, existing inscriptions and seals are retained. They are neither the learner’s competition entries nor AI reinterpretations generated in this task. Titles follow the legible poem titles on the attachments. Liu Zongyuan, Du Mu and the other names in the inscriptions identify poets and must not be treated as the illustrators. Complete observation notes

Continuous open space

Rivers, village roads, gaps around stone steps and wall surfaces connect into pale passages running through the frame; large open areas are themselves major shapes. Plant detail concentrates on both sides, often guiding the eye along angular or S-shaped routes. Open space does not mean drawing nothing: a few water lines, fallen petals and reflections can suggest materials.

Elevated viewpoints and layers

Most images use a relatively high viewpoint, with roofs, steps, canopies and banks overlapping in successive layers. Clear size differences, overlaps and diagonal edges establish depth. Space retains a flat decorative quality at the same time. Small Pond shifts to a close-up near the lotus leaves, varying the series’ scale; there is no need to force every photograph into a view from above.

The framework of canopies

Massed canopies and dark branching structures register first, followed by dense leaf dots, broken edges and tiny highlights. Edges and texture

Building and branch edges are relatively definite, while clusters of dots break up canopy edges. Matte color areas, fine grain and slight variations in paper-like tone combine into the appearance of printed illustration. These are visible effects: “paper-like” and “printmaking-like” do not establish a verified material or production process.

Small actions tell stories

A person fishing, a herder pointing the way, swallows flying, a traveler climbing steps, a dragonfly resting on a lotus tip and a flowering branch crossing a wall are all actions readable at a glance. Figures are usually small, yet object relationships remain clear. Action, position and local color contrast determine the focus, which need not be the largest building.

Poetry and layout

Large titles sit over dark canopies, fine vertical verses occupy pale open areas and red seals create small rhythmic accents. Text and image distribute density together. All inscriptions and seals in the original attachments are retained. By default, photo reinterpretation borrows only the painting language. For a separate poetry poster, plan text placement first, then add verified verses with a layout tool.

Appreciation and choices

River Snow’s limited colors and solitary boat, Small Pond’s rolled lotus leaf and dragonfly, and An Unsuccessful Visit to a Garden’s branch crossing the wall have the clearest relationships to their inscribed verses. Wuyi Lane’s lavish residence and Night Mooring by Maple Bridge’s broad warm reds and bright ground emphasize decoration and landmarks more, with relatively less chill, night atmosphere or contrast between past and present. These are judgments from this appreciation exercise; before reusing the approach, decide what feeling you actually want to express.

Choices when reusing these features

Suitable for turning the learner’s photographs of riverbanks, gardens, flowering trees, paths after rain or lotus ponds into narrative illustrations with open space. First have her identify a real observation or action, then retain the original photograph’s main path, shoreline, relationships among people and viewpoint. Simplify existing foliage into broad color masses, add structure with fine lines and dotted leaves, and finally select one color relationship: blue-green and vermilion, blue-gray snow, peach pink, or lotus-pond greens. There is no need to add temples, boats or figures in historical dress that are absent from the image.

Tendencies to avoid

Avoid merely applying a blue-green filter, turning every leaf into the same amount of noise, or forcing a narrow crop to imitate vertical posters. Do not let red flowers and lantern light across the image overwhelm the intended quiet and chill, or replace an entire real contemporary scene with historical buildings. Relationships among the poems’ figures, objects and seasons require deliberate choices; placing a poem on an image cannot substitute for visual expression.

中文

将输入照片完整改绘为具有古诗意境的新中式装饰山水插画，参考本组的细线勾勒、团簇树冠、哑光色块、纸本颗粒与连续留白。照片决定内容、构图、视角和画幅，参考图只提供视觉语言。保留主体数量、人物身份与表情、动作、原有衣着、物件关系、建筑和地形结构、前后遮挡及原始比例；不要把现代场景替换成古代。沿照片已有的河道、小径、天空、地面或墙面组织较安静的浅色区域，以两侧已有的植被和建筑形成疏密对照；如果原图没有贯通通道，就保留原有空隙，不另造河流或道路。先把树冠与叶簇归纳为清楚的大色团，再用近黑的分枝、细密但分区有序的叶点、碎边和少量亮斑建立层次；砖瓦、石阶、栏杆、叶脉以节制细线表达。减少摄影式光滑渐变，保留必要明暗与空间重叠，整幅统一为绘画语言。按原照片的季节、光线和情绪选一组色彩：常态用米纸白、墨青绿与瓦青，少量朱砂红作焦点；冬景改用蓝灰、近黑与米白；花海可用桃粉主调和少量青绿；荷塘以深浅绿配少量红。不要为配色凭空添加雪、红叶、花朵、灯笼或改变天气。让原有的一个小动作或观察成为故事焦点，在人物面部、手和关键物件附近减少纹理，保持清楚可辨；明暗与色彩强度服从要表达的感受。水面只用疏朗细纹和概括倒影提示质感，纸面颗粒轻薄，不覆盖细节。不强制改为俯视或狭长竖幅，不导入参考中的寺庙、酒楼、舟船、牧童、燕子、蜻蜓或古装人物，不新增诗句、标题、印章、签名、边框和水印。避免均匀噪点、荧光色、厚重油画堆积、写实 3D、摄影贴片及仅加调色滤镜。

English

Repaint the entire input photograph as a contemporary Chinese-style decorative landscape illustration with the atmosphere of classical Chinese poetry, borrowing the visual language of these references: fine contour drawing, clustered tree canopies, matte color fields, subtle paper grain and connected areas of open space. Let the photograph determine content, composition, viewpoint and frame; use the references only for visual language. Preserve subject count, recognizable identities and expressions, actions, existing clothing, object relationships, architecture, terrain, occlusion and proportions; do not replace a contemporary scene with an ancient one. Use existing water, paths, sky, ground or walls as calmer light areas, framed by vegetation and architecture already present. If the input has no continuous passage, keep its existing gaps instead of inventing a river or road. Establish broad, readable canopy masses first, then articulate them with near-black branching structures, regionally organized leaf stippling, broken edges and restrained highlights. Describe brickwork, roof tiles, stone steps, railings and leaf veins with economical fine lines. Reduce smooth photographic gradients while retaining necessary shading, overlap and readable depth in a consistent illustrated treatment. Choose one palette variant to suit the actual season, light and mood: warm paper ivory, deep blue-green and turquoise with limited vermilion accents; blue-gray, near-black and ivory for a winter scene; peach-pink masses with small blue-green accents for blossom scenes; or layered greens with sparse red for a lotus pond. Do not invent snow, red foliage, flowers or lanterns, or change the weather to force a palette. Let one existing small action or observed detail carry the story. Reduce texture near faces, hands and important objects so they remain legible, and let contrast and color intensity serve the intended feeling. Suggest water only with sparse fine ripples and simplified reflections; keep paper grain light and subtle so it does not cover details. Do not force an elevated viewpoint or a narrow vertical crop. Do not import temples, taverns, boats, young herders, swallows, dragonflies or figures in historical dress from the references. Add no poem, title, seal, signature, border or watermark. Avoid uniform visual noise, fluorescent color, heavy impasto, realistic 3D, photographic patches and color filtering without redrawing.
